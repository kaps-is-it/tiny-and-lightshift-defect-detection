# Industrial imaging and machine learning for tiny defects under changing light

The [public-split report](results/experiments.md) records the completed comparisons and their configurations.

This is a research plan for the MVTec AD 2 project, with a separate path for a physical inspection station. It uses the public dataset and the project’s measured results as evidence, checked the official leaderboard, and ranks the experiments by how directly they address the observed failures. The numerical examples below identify engineering constraints. Improvements still need to be measured.

## 1. What we can infer from our own results

The baseline has three failure modes. A defect can have insufficient contrast in the captured pixels, resizing or the feature grid can suppress evidence that the camera did record, and a part score can rank glare or a rim above an actual defect. Each needs a different intervention.

- On public Can, tiled PatchCore reached 39.2% mean AU-PRO at a 5% pixel false-positive integration cap, up from roughly zero for full-frame resizing. The tiled run missed all 90 defective captures at its original validation-derived image threshold. The localization gain establishes that spatial detail matters. The part misses expose a separate scoring problem.
- Removing broad heatmap responses helped at the tested operating points on Can and Sheet Metal. The same fixed rule missed 19 more defective Fabric captures and 28 more defective Wall Plugs captures, while falsely rejecting fewer good captures. Neither transfer category showed an improvement in misses on its tiny-only subgroup. Treat a local score as one signal to test. Preserve the raw map as a reference.
- Reduced SuperADD at native scale improved overall AU-PRO from 6.4% to 11.1% and reduced tiny-region overlap from 17.2% to 9.1%. Changing scale alone can improve a headline average while hurting the regions the project cares most about. This comparison used ViT-S/16 and a reduced training bank. The full ViT-H+ configuration remains open. [Project baseline](https://github.com/kaps-is-it/tiny-defects-under-changing-light#first-results) · [Scoring transfer](https://github.com/kaps-is-it/tiny-defects-under-changing-light/blob/main/results/hybrid_transfer.md) · [Reduced SuperADD](https://github.com/kaps-is-it/tiny-defects-under-changing-light/blob/main/results/superadd.md)

The MVTec AD 2 paper describes separate interventions for exposure and lighting geometry. The public captures let us compare views of the same scene. The private regular and mixed sets are a blinded benchmark. A 2026 IEEE study found that 30.9–46.1% of calibrated decisions flipped in its paired private regular/mixed comparisons across five tested configurations. This measures sensitivity of decisions to the image change. The private labels were unavailable to that local comparison. In the local Can data, an exploratory visibility proxy divided the absolute difference between the median grayscale value inside the regular-light mask and that in a 4–12-pixel surrounding annulus by the annulus's robust grayscale spread. Reusing those mask coordinates across views, the proxy improved in 13 of 15 scenes under Shift 1, with a median paired increase of 0.33. A lighting change can improve local defect contrast while generating nuisance reflections elsewhere in the image. [MVTec AD 2 journal paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02743-0) · [IEEE Access reliability study](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3735579)

## 3. Calculations that change what we should build

### Spatial sampling and pooling

I checked the 15 regular-light defective Can masks. The images are 2,232 × 1,024. Total marked area per image is 25–450 pixels, with a median of 129. There are 16 eight-connected regions. The median shorter side of their axis-aligned bounding boxes is 9.5 pixels. A distance-to-background estimate gives a median inscribed thickness of about 5.6 pixels. Both are raster proxies. Oblique scratches can be thinner than their bounding boxes, and physical width requires a calibrated field of view.

- At 256 pixels wide the 9.5-pixel box extent becomes about 1.09 pixels and the 5.6-pixel thickness proxy becomes about 0.64 pixels. At 512 pixels wide they become 2.18 and 1.29 pixels. The 25–450-pixel native mask areas shrink to about 0.33–5.92 pixel-equivalents at 256 wide.
- The current CNN’s finest grid has stride 8. On a full-frame 256 input, adjacent grid locations are about 70 native pixels apart horizontally on Can. Native-size tiles reduce that distance to 8 pixels. A stride-4 detail branch would reduce it further. A native DINOv3 ViT/16 token spans a 16-pixel patch. These figures describe sampling geometry. Detection can respond to sub-cell evidence and depends on contrast, receptive field, and position.
- Averaging a fixed 0.01% of native pixels uses 229 values on Can and 502 on Fabric or Wall Plugs. The median Can mask covers 129 pixels. Even an ideally ordered top tail would mix that many defect pixels with 100 other pixels. This can diminish small responses. Compare one moderate and one very small tail and a connected-region score, calibrating each score separately from normal images.
- A 5% false-positive-pixel cap on a roughly 2.29-million-pixel clean Can image corresponds to about 114,000 pixels if applied to that image alone. AU-PRO integrates over a pooled range of pixel false-positive rates. Record region recall at stricter fixed pixel false-positive rates or false marked area, alongside segmentation F1 and image decisions. [Local data inventory](https://github.com/kaps-is-it/tiny-defects-under-changing-light/blob/main/PROJECT_PLAN.md) · [MVTec metric definition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02743-0) · [Optical resolution](https://www.edmundoptics.com/knowledge-center/application-notes/imaging/resolution/)

### Compute and operating thresholds

The repo’s 256-pixel windows with a 192-pixel maximum step cover Can in 60 tiles and Fabric in 143. Reducing the step to 128 increases those counts to 119 and 285. For Can at stride 8 the 60 tiles produce 61,440 overlapping queries. A dense FP32 distance matrix against 4,096 reference vectors would use 960 MiB even before intermediate tensors. A chunk of 2,048 queries needs 32 MiB for the distances. Halving feature stride gives roughly four times as many queries. This makes a narrow detail branch, a compact coreset and streamed matching practical choices on the project’s 8 GiB GPU. Time the full original image, including decoding where relevant, preprocessing, stitching, scoring and transfer. [Project implementation](https://github.com/kaps-is-it/tiny-defects-under-changing-light/blob/main/run_experiment.py) · [PatchCore paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)

The maximum grows more likely to hit a spurious response as the number of opportunities goes up. As an illustration, 60 independent windows each with a 1% nuisance crossing rate would yield 1 − 0.99^60 = 45.3% image-level crossings. Real overlapping windows are correlated. Calibrate the whole part score on good images with the same tile layout instead of using that independence assumption.

For one-sided 95% binomial limits, zero false rejects among 12 independent good scenes still allows a false-reject rate of about 22%. Zero among four allows about 53%. About 299 independent good scenes with zero false rejects would be required to put that upper limit under 1%. This assumes independent samples representative of the deployment condition. Six views of one part stay one scene for uncertainty. A 46-image normal calibration set also gives a minimum split-conformal p-value of 1/47 ≈ 2.13%. Fine-grained low-risk claims require more good calibration data or a justified and validated parametric model. [Conformal outlier testing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)

### Physical imaging examples

Object-space sampling equals field width divided by horizontal pixels. A hypothetical 50 µm scratch across a 100 mm field spans 1.12 pixels at 2,232 pixels wide. A four-pixel design target would require 8,000 horizontal samples at that field width or a field of about 28 mm at 2,232 pixels. Four pixels is an engineering hypothesis to verify with a defect sample and measured lens/sensor contrast. These values are illustrative. The public masks do not provide millimetres.

Motion blur in pixels is approximately speed × exposure time ÷ object-space pixel pitch. At 0.5 m/s and 25 µm per pixel, one pixel of smear allows about 50 µs. Encoder-synchronized line scan at that pitch requires 20,000 lines/s. At 4,096 pixels and two bytes per sample, that is about 164 MB/s before overhead. These estimates help choose imaging geometry and strobe timing early. [Lens and sensor matching](https://www.edmundoptics.com/knowledge-center/application-notes/imaging/sensors-and-lenses/) · [Teledyne motion-blur guidance](https://www.teledynevisionsolutions.com/support/support-center/technical-guidance/iis/determining-the-degree-of-motion-blur-in-an-image/) · [Basler line-scan applications](https://docs.baslerweb.com/line-scan-gige-use-cases)

## 4. Ranked experiments using the images we have

Within each row, freeze the settings before evaluating the same held-out scenes. An independent input branch gives us a clean ablation and preserves access to the original signal.

| Order | Solution | First test and what it can tell us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Measure where evidence disappears | Audit mask width, clipping and local contrast at native, 256, and 512 scale. Use a surrounding background annulus and retain both polarities. Align each paired view and log visible defects, bright reflections and model hotspots separately. This tells us whether the problem starts before the detector. |
| 2 | High-resolution early CNN features | Keep tiled PatchCore as the control. Add a stride-4 detail map from an early ResNet block. Measure it on its own and with the current stride-8 map. Then test DINOv3 ConvNeXt-Tiny stage 0 or 1 at the native intermediate grid as a compact pretrained challenger. A multi-scale oriented gradient or ridge bank is another lightweight detail branch for thin scratches. Hold the tiling and reference budget fixed initially, then measure runtime and memory. |
| 3 | Fine-detail and context fusion | Compare the early CNN map with the current CNN context map or a frozen DINOv3 map. Restore their original coordinates. Scale each map against good validation scores before combining maps. Test each branch and one fixed fusion rule. Fit any final score thresholds again on separate good validation images. Coarse confidence alone should not gate access to the detail search. |
| 4 | Improve normal patch coverage | Compare the uniform bank with PatchCore-style greedy or approximate coreset selection at the same budget. Try matching by registered position or neighborhood where the scene layout is stable. Keep global matching as a control on varying labels or overlapping parts. Measure whether edges or printed detail dominate errors. |
| 5 | Reproduce published comparators | Run the released SuperADD on Can after retrieving its whole bank. The project has the ViT-H+ weights and a partial bank transfer. Try CAT or ISVL++ as a separate targeted comparator. Pin code and weights. Match public scenes and compute metrics with our evaluator, documenting any deviations. |
| 6 | Use a separate, size-aware part score | Compare raw maximum, small top tails, and connected evidence across multiple sizes. For example, use a peak for tiny marks and a coherent region score for holes or missing material. Normalize each evidence score on good examples and recalibrate the combined result at the part level. Evaluate recall at the same false-reject budget. |
| 7 | Photometric normalization as a parallel view | Test exposure normalization, mild gamma, gradients, and one restrained local illumination correction such as log-luminance background subtraction or Retinex. Keep the RGB/native comparison. Gradients may suppress smooth illumination and make scratches stand out. CLAHE or aggressive high-pass filtering can amplify surface texture, noise, or glare edges and weaken broad defects. Score gradient polarity sensibly and check originals for lost signals. |
| 8 | More realistic training augmentation | Extend the existing brightness/contrast baseline with constrained gamma or per-channel shifts, low-frequency gradients, and separately tested small specular highlights. Set strength from known camera or train/validation behavior. Track whether robustness gains erase fine defects. Use paired captures for consistency learning only in a separate study with verified independent support parts. |
| 9 | Student–teacher and reconstruction challengers | Compare EfficientAD as a latency-oriented option and reverse distillation or INP-Former as a different model family. The official paper’s and vendors’ timing conditions differ from ours. Benchmark at identical image and tile coverage. Compare the fine-region recall and low-FPR localization of their outputs. |
| 10 | Synthetic-defect supervision | Use a shallow segmentation head with controlled scratch, pit, hole or missing-print overlays on good source images, with realistic widths and smooth blending. A DRAEM-style model or a VAND-style segmentation head is a reference. Hold out synthetic generators and test only on real independent scenes. The test can reveal if the model learns paste boundaries instead of defect evidence. |
| 11 | Supervised and few-shot specialist models | If real labeled production defects become available, compare a high-resolution segmentation head or detection model trained on them. Separate by SKU, physical part and lot. Keep normal-only results as a separate setting. Few real labels can be a useful practical benchmark even when a normal-only leaderboard protocol has no such labels. |
| 12 | Compare multi-frame evidence with one frame | For explicitly available paired captures, compare calibrated max, averaging, or quality-weighted fusion. Use capture-specific masks for single-image metrics and a predeclared scene-level policy for the multi-frame test. Any analysis that chooses the best view after seeing the defect measures an upper bound. |

Evidence behind these directions includes the PatchCore coreset, position-conditioned patch modeling in PNI, DINOv3 dense representations, learned prototype reconstruction, synthetic segmentation heads, and compact student–teacher models. None establishes the best scale or score for our Can images. [PatchCore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265) · [PNI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12634) · [DINOv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10104) · [INP-Former](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02424) · [EfficientAD](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/html/Batzner_EfficientAD_Accurate_Visual_Anomaly_Detection_at_Millisecond-Level_Latencies_WACV_2024_paper.html) · [Reverse distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10703) · [DRAEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610) · [Multiscale Retinex](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18282987/) · [DINOv3 ConvNeXt implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/blob/main/dinov3/models/convnext.py) · [Memory-efficient industrial tiling](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/VAND/html/Rolih_Divide_and_Conquer_High-Resolution_Industrial_Anomaly_Detection_via_Memory_Efficient_CVPRW_2024_paper.html)

Related work limits the novelty claim. An independent project already combined DINOv3 and local CNN features with overlapping tiling, synthetic-defect supervision, and photometric experiments. Its linked official row scored 60.73% mixed mean AU-PRO across categories and 10.11% on mixed Can. Its report found no transfer from its tested photometric remedies to moving Can highlights. Our strongest research claim would need evidence that finer spatial features improve recovery of the smallest defects and a locked operating threshold transfers beyond development scenes. [Visual Inspector technical report](https://github.com/ThanhDatVN/MVTec-Visual-Inspector/blob/main/reports/00-final-report.md) · [Official leaderboard feed](https://www.benchmark.mvtec.com/v1/leaderboard)

Diffusion reconstruction, learned super-resolution, and general-purpose vision-language models are lower-priority controls. Reconstruction may remove a subtle flaw or invent texture. Super-resolution may change the appearance of small scratches. A language model may help characterize an alert, while precise localization still needs spatial evidence and rigorous masks. Compare against native input and count hallucinated and erased defects before claiming gains.

## 5. Lighting robustness, calibration, and operator workflow

1. Detect an acquisition change directly. Use known-good references or a fixed reference target. Track luminance quantiles, clipped-pixel fraction, spatial brightness gradients, sharpness, and camera or light metadata when available. Start with an exponentially weighted control chart or a simple sustained alarm. Evaluate normal and shifted batches for false alerts, shift recall, and time to detect. Image statistics can flag a change. Labeled results are needed to know whether inspection quality also degraded. [NIST EWMA reference](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc314.htm)
2. Repeat small-reference recalibration with explicit scene exclusion. Start with the project’s paired median offset with two or four verified-good parts. Compare budget and repeatability across several predeclared splits. Adding a constant per light moves a threshold and leaves within-light AUROC mathematically unchanged. Compare that with a small, capped target-light reference bank or robust feature correction if the shift changes how defects rank.
3. Evaluate SPARC with scene-disjoint support. The preprint estimates a per-location nuisance subspace with up to eight verified normals, without gradient updates. Its gains depend on the detector and setup. It reports smaller ranking gains and mixed localization when calibration scenes are fully excluded from evaluation. Use the same support count for alternatives and check alignment, drift, and whether real defect features also get removed. [SPARC preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18585v1)
4. Calibrate for a decision budget. Set thresholds from good production-like data, compare missed defects at a matched false-reject rate, and report actual counts as well as confidence intervals. Conformal p-values can provide an auditable nominal baseline under exchangeability. A new lighting distribution can break that assumption. A score or an anomaly p-value does not estimate the probability of a defect.
5. Test pass, reject, and review as an operational experiment. Compare review for image-quality failures, borderline scores, or branch disagreement at the same manual review budget, along with a random-review control. Count defective parts automatically passed over all defective parts, false rejects, and workload. For example, with 0.1% defects, 95% recall, and a 2% false-positive rate, only about 4.5% of auto-alerts would be genuine defects under that illustrative prevalence. [Selective classification](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)

The existing Can results already support a ranking-first approach. From the stored scores I enumerated all 495 ways to pick four of the 12 good reference scenes. In each comparison, all views of those four scenes were excluded from both sets of decisions. On full-frame Shift 2, the median offset cut false rejects in 453 sets and increased misses in all 495. On Shift 3, those counts were 485 and 495, with a median trade of two fewer false rejects and five extra missed defective scenes. The overlapping reference sets measure sensitivity to the choice of reference parts. A separate 2026 preprint, ShiftSplit-AD, found that suppressing broad residuals improved ranking on a shifted benchmark and hurt held-out clean categories, which reinforces the case for preserving the raw branch. [ShiftSplit-AD preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27610v1#S5)

Evaluate these separately from the official single-image benchmark. Its test labels and scenes stay out of training, hyperparameter choices, and model updates. Verified shifted normals belong in a separately labeled commissioning study, with every view of each support scene excluded from evaluation. [MVTec AD 2 benchmark protocol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02743-0)

## 6. Hardware path for a real inspection station

| Technology | Good first application | Main trade-off to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enclosure, rigid fixture and triggered lighting | Stabilize the viewpoint, exposure, gain and white balance. Use a global shutter and short synchronized strobe for moving parts. | Measure ambient leakage, repeatability, motion smear and focus. |
| Dark-field grazing lights | Surface scratches and raised material. Try two orthogonal directions on sheet metal and separate diffuse and grazing captures on cans. | A scratch can respond differently by orientation. Curvature can create glare at edges. |
| Dome or diffuse front light, coaxial bright field, transmitted light | Diffuse light for curved printed parts and fabric. Coaxial lighting as a complementary view for flat reflective surfaces. Backlighting for holes and silhouettes where geometry allows. | Each light emphasizes a different physical defect. Preserve separate views when their information is complementary. |
| Cross polarization or wavelength selection | Test against specular glare or ambient contamination. A matched narrowband filter is an option where colour is unnecessary. | Polarization reduces signal and can also suppress the reflection that reveals a scratch. Colour defects need a channel that preserves their contrast. |
| Calibrated dark/flat field and HDR | On raw data, correct fixed shading using dark and flat frames. Compare bracketed exposures or single-acquisition dual-gain industrial sensors for clipping. | Multi-exposure capture needs registration and time. Mosaiced HDR and polarization sensors change per-channel spatial sampling. A clipped value in an existing file carries limited usable contrast information. |
| Lens/sensor matching and greater magnification | Determine field of view, working distance, depth of field and blur from a calibrated defect sample. Use a telecentric lens where consistent magnification or geometric gauging warrants it. | Resolution is the combination of optics, sensor, illumination and noise. Use EMVA 1288 measurements and a contrast target at the relevant size. |
| Multi-light fusion and photometric stereo | First test two or more complementary views. Use calibrated independent light directions and fixed alignment for photometric stereo. | Classic diffuse-reflectance assumptions can fail on shiny metal. Surface gradients or relative height require calibrated acquisition. AD 2 exposure and spotlight changes are a different dataset design. |
| Close-up view, 360° inspection, or encoder-driven line scan | Rotate or surround cans to cover hidden surfaces. For moving sheet or fabric, consider line-scan and time-delay integration when speed and light justify it. | More acquisition time, synchronization, data bandwidth, and registration work. |
| Deflectometry, depth or spectral imaging | Deflectometry for slopes on mirror-like surfaces. Laser or confocal methods for measurable height. Spectral imaging if contaminants and substrate differ materially. | These are different measurement tasks. They need appropriate sensors and evidence that the target has observable slope, height, or spectral contrast. |

Factory-facing guidance is grounded in published acquisition methods, vendor reference designs and sensor specifications. A separate dataset such as Real-IAD D³ provides synchronized RGB, photometric-stereo normals, and 3D measurements for real multimodal testing. [MVTec acquisition setups](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02743-0) · [KEYENCE lighting selection](https://www.keyence.com/products/vision/resources/vision-resources/basics-of-lighting-selection.jsp) · [Basler shading correction](https://docs.baslerweb.com/shading-correction) · [Sony industrial HDR](https://www.sony-semicon.com/en/technology/industry/pregius.html) · [EMVA 1288](https://www.emva.org/standards-technology/emva-1288/) · [HALCON photometric stereo](https://www.mvtec.com/doc/halcon/2211/en/photometric_stereo.html) · [Line-scan deflectometry study](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.596567) · [Real-IAD D³](https://realiad4ad.github.io/Real-IAD_D3/)

A cost example follows from the project’s 168 ms Can tiled baseline. Four serial inferences would use about 672 ms per part before data movement or acquisition. Asking for one additional inference on 15% of parts would use about 193 ms expected compute per part. For a cascade with 30 ms initial compute and 168 ms for full fallback, a 100 ms average compute budget permits fallback on about 42% of parts. Our current 256-wide branch is a poor tiny-defect gate. A cheap native-detail scan, an image-quality signal, or externally defined product coverage must be validated before skipping a fine inspection.

## 7. Order for the project

1. Start on Can with the visibility audit and the same frozen raw tiled baseline. Run early detail alone, existing context alone, and their normalized fusion. Keep training subjects, windows, calibration split and evaluator identical. Include native ROI close-ups of misses, glare and seams.
2. Do the independent comparator work alongside that. Complete the released SuperADD Can run, and use CAT or ISVL++ as a separate comparison if their released resources fit the hardware. Document whether architecture, reference size, precision or input shape changes.
3. Evaluate the leading maps with a distinct part score. Use normal-only thresholds, record segmentation F1 at a locked pixel threshold, and report defect escape and false reject at matched budgets alongside AU-PRO. Report region recall or overlap in size groups and record p50/p95 latency and peak GPU memory.
4. Check transfer on Sheet Metal, Fabric and Wall Plugs with previous results identified as development context. Lock the candidate before comparing untouched public categories: Fruit Jelly, Rice, Vial and Walnuts. Bootstrap by scene, keep all views together and report regular vs changed conditions. Optional 2D re-imaging and verified-normal calibration belong on their own tracks.
5. After the settings are fixed, verify local packaging for the private splits. Package the expected score maps and optional binary masks. Use the labeled public captures for quantitative evaluation. Add a separate portfolio demo with paired evidence that shows a recovered defect and a failure. [MVTec benchmark format](https://benchmark.mvtec.com/submit)

The first experiment to run after research is the controlled stride-4 detail and context ablation on Can. A second, small factorial comparison can cross the selected detail design with the source-only lighting augmentation, varying geometry and photometric robustness separately. It directly addresses the spatial scale measured in the masks, leaves room to isolate score and threshold effects, and fits the existing tiled detector workflow.
